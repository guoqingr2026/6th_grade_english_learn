# Basic Grammar

Understanding the structure of sentences is key to communicating clearly in English.

## 1. Basic Sentence Structure (S-V-O)
English sentences generally follow the **Subject - Verb - Object** structure.

* **Subject (S)**: Who or what is doing the action.
* **Verb (V)**: The action.
* **Object (O)**: The person or thing receiving the action.

**Examples:**
* *I (S) eat (V) apples (O).*
* *She (S) reads (V) a book (O).*
* *They (S) play (V) soccer (O).*

---

## 2. The Verb "To Be" (Am, Is, Are)
The verb "to be" is the most common verb in English. It is used to describe a state of being, identity, or location.

### Present Tense
| Pronoun | Verb "To Be" | Example |
|---------|--------------|---------|
| I | am | I **am** a student. |
| You | are | You **are** my friend. |
| He / She / It | is | She **is** happy. / It **is** hot. |
| We | are | We **are** learning. |
| They | are | They **are** outside. |

---

## 3. Simple Present Tense
We use the simple present tense to talk about habits, routines, and general facts.

**Rule for "He / She / It":** Add **-s** or **-es** to the verb.

**Examples:**
* I **work** every day.
* She **works** every day. *(Notice the added 's')*
* We **watch** movies on weekends.
* He **watches** movies on weekends. *(Notice the added 'es')*

---

## 4. Singular and Plural Nouns
Usually, to make a word plural (more than one), you just add **-s**.

* One cat → Two cat**s**
* One car → Three car**s**

**Exceptions:**
* Words ending in *s, x, z, ch, sh*: add **-es** (box → boxes, bus → buses)
* Words ending in a consonant + *y*: change *y* to *i* and add **-es** (city → cities)
* Irregular plurals: man → men, woman → women, child → children, tooth → teeth.
